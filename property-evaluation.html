Why it matters

Every portal shows you what people asked for.

An asking price is an opinion with a phone number attached. It is not evidence, and a valuation built out of a page of them inherits every bit of optimism in it. What a property actually sold for is a different number, and it is the one that matters.

  • We price from transactions we were part of or can verify, not from listings.
  • Every comparable used is named in the report, with why it was chosen.
  • If the evidence is thin for your property type, the report says so rather than guessing confidently.

How it works

Five steps, and none of them happen at a desk.

The order matters as much as the steps. Inspecting a property after you have decided what it is worth is not inspection, it is confirmation.

Step 01

The brief

What the evaluation is for changes what it has to withstand. Selling, buying, a bank, a partnership, a division of assets and an insurance figure are five different documents, and we would rather ask than assume.

Step 02

The inspection

In person, room by room, with photographs. Aspect, floor, view, natural light, the state of the finishes, what the building's common parts look like, and whether the parking is real parking. None of this is visible from a portal listing and all of it moves the number.

Step 03

The comparables

Transactions in the same district, as close as possible in size, age and specification. We say how many we found and how good a match each one is — three close comparables are worth more than fifteen loose ones, and a report that does not tell you which it had is hiding something.

Step 04

The adjustments

No two properties are the same, so each comparable is adjusted for the ways it differs — floor, size, condition, finish, outdoor space, service charge. Every adjustment is written down. This is the part that makes the number arguable in the useful sense.

Step 05

The opinion

A figure, a realistic range around it, and what would have to be true to reach the top of that range. If we think it should be marketed at a different number from what it is worth, we say both and explain why.

What you get

Four things arrive, and you own all of them.

One document, delivered as a document — not a link to a portal you lose access to.

01 — Value

The figure, and the range

One number, and the band we would expect it to transact within. A single figure with no range is a guess dressed up, and a range so wide it cannot be wrong is useless.

02 — Comparables

The evidence

Each comparable listed with its district, size, date and how it was adjusted. You can check our reasoning, and so can anyone you show it to.

03 — Condition

The condition note

What we saw, with photographs, and what it is costing you. Often the most useful page in the document, because it is the one that lists things you can actually change.

04 — Recommendations

What would move it

The work that would pay for itself before sale and the work that would not. Most owners are about to spend money on the second kind.
