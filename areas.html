District 01 · south-west

Abdoun

68 on the bookApartments and villas60–720 m²

The embassy quarter, and the most expensive address in the city. Wide, quiet streets on a plateau south-west of the centre, walled villas next to low-rise apartment buildings, and almost every diplomatic residence in Amman inside a ten-minute radius.

It is where most people who arrive with a posting end up looking first, which is why two thirds of what we hold is here. The trade is space and security for the fact that you will drive everywhere.

District 02 · the spine

The Circles

14 on the bookApartments80–330 m²

Amman is navigated by roundabouts, and the ones along Zahran Street are the oldest part of the west. The 2nd is Jabal Amman proper — 1930s stone, Rainbow Street below it, embassies and cafés on foot. By the 4th you are in hotel and ministry territory; by the 5th it is residential again and quieter.

Buildings here are older and smaller than Abdoun's, and the flats have thicker walls and better proportions. It is the part of the city you can live in without a car.

District 03 · west

Swefieh

10 on the bookApartments95–220 m²

The commercial west: Wakalat Street, the shops, the restaurants, and the densest concentration of anything-you-need in the city. Residential floors sit above and behind all of it, which means you can walk to a supermarket at ten at night — rare here.

You pay for convenience rather than land, so the same money buys a noticeably larger flat than in Abdoun. It is louder, and that is the whole trade.

District 04 · north-central

Shmeisani

9 on the bookApartments80–200 m²

The business district — banks, head offices, the older hotels — built out in the seventies and eighties and still the shortest commute to most white-collar work in the city. Apartment buildings sit directly above and between the offices.

It empties at the weekend, which people either like very much or not at all. Good value if your day is spent in an office here; less so if it is not.

District 05 · east, on the hill

Jabal Al Weibdeh

7 on the bookApartments70–220 m²

The oldest residential hill in the city and the one people are most sentimental about: 1940s–60s stone, narrow streets, stairs everywhere, galleries, small cafés, and the only part of Amman that rewards walking without a destination.

Flats are smaller and older, and the buildings rarely have parking or a lift. It attracts a particular kind of tenant and keeps them for years.

District 06 · west

Um Uthaina

6 on the bookApartments80–250 m²

Between Swefieh and the upper Circles, and quieter than either. Mostly newer apartment buildings on residential streets, with the shops of Swefieh close enough to walk to and far enough not to hear.

It gets overlooked because it has no landmark and no name recognition abroad. That is usually reflected in the price.

District 07 · south of Abdoun

Dair Ghbar

4 on the bookApartments and villas100–1,200 m²

Abdoun's quieter southern neighbour. Larger plots, more garden, fewer people, and the biggest single property on our book sits here. It is residential and almost nothing else.

The right answer for someone who wants Abdoun's calm without Abdoun's price, and the wrong one for anybody who wants to walk anywhere.
